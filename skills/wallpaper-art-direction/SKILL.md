# Wallpaper art direction

Turn the request into one image a person would want behind their desktop icons. Make every phrase visually actionable, especially for smaller prompt models.

## Build the prompt

1. State one primary subject or environment first.
2. Add one visible event or state: lightning splitting mist, fire flowing like silk, rain clearing over clay, sunlight cutting through moss, or steam rising where lava meets ice.
3. Name the medium precisely: cinematic landscape photography, tactile paper-cut relief, editorial gouache, macro botanical photography, painterly realism, or premium abstract 3D.
4. Describe the requested composition using foreground, middle distance, horizon, focal placement, and areas of visual rest. Use widescreen only when orientation is unspecified or explicitly landscape.
5. Preserve explicitly requested colors. Otherwise choose a restrained palette of three to five named colors, with one luminous accent when appropriate.
6. Name two or three physical textures that the image should visibly contain.
7. Finish with the intended mood and a clean image-only requirement.

Use concrete nouns and visible properties. Prefer "wet moss on black basalt beneath yellow sun shafts" to "a beautiful magical forest." Give one direction rather than alternatives.

## Palette families

Select a family that supports the subject instead of defaulting to blue and yellow:

- Storm: midnight blue, ultraviolet, dusty violet, cold white lightning.
- Fire: near-black, crimson, vermilion, ember orange, small gold sparks.
- Living earth: forest green, chartreuse moss, volcanic black, warm yellow light.
- Dry earth: terracotta, rust red, raw umber, charcoal, muted copper.
- Water: emerald, teal, limestone beige, pale gold sunbeams.
- Botanical: plum, violet, magenta, wine red, silver droplets.
- Mineral: clay red, oxidized copper, turquoise, sand, deep brown.
- Quiet light: powder blue, cream, pale lemon, soft gray.

## Texture and atmosphere

Use texture as part of the subject, not decoration. Useful combinations include:

- mist with layered mountain silhouettes;
- rain on velvet petals or dark leaves;
- smoke, steam, and glowing fissures around fire;
- cracked clay with oxidized copper and mineral pools;
- translucent ice, liquid glass, or shallow reflective water;
- handmade paper fibers with delicate relief shadows;
- moss, wet basalt, limestone, sand, or weathered concrete.

Keep atmospheric depth legible: a clear foreground texture, a strong middle-distance subject, and a softened background. Let mist, rain, smoke, or light connect those layers.

## Compose for a desktop

- Default to a full-bleed 16:9 landscape composition unless the user explicitly requests another orientation.
- Place one strong focal event near, but not always at, the center.
- Keep far-left and far-right regions calmer and lower contrast for icons and widgets.
- Use scale deliberately: a tiny cabin, train, chair, lamp, or observatory can make a large environment feel immense.
- Avoid dense detail everywhere. Pair one intricate region with broad areas of rest.

## Lower-capability model guardrails

- Keep one subject, one action, one medium, and one palette.
- Use short declarative clauses in visual order.
- Avoid metaphors unless immediately translated into appearance, such as "flames flowing like silk underwater."
- Avoid brand names, artist names, alternatives, nested scenes, and contradictory lighting.
- State "image only, no text, logo, watermark, or border" once at the end.
- Usually target 80–180 words; allow shorter simple scenes and at most 250 words when material and atmosphere need detail. Preserve explicit subjects, styles, colors, exclusions, and orientation. Choose a coherent artistic direction for unspecified details.

## Prompt pattern

Write the final prompt in this order:

`primary scene, visible event, precise medium, requested composition and focal placement, lighting and atmosphere, named palette, physical textures, mood, image-only constraint`

Example:

`Ancient moss-covered basalt columns in a dense forest, warm sun shafts cutting through drifting mist, atmospheric fine-art nature photography, panoramic full-bleed composition with strong vertical rhythm in the middle distance and quiet dark edges, deep forest green, chartreuse moss, volcanic black, and soft yellow light, wet stone and humid foliage textures, sacred and restorative, image only with no text, logo, watermark, or border.`
